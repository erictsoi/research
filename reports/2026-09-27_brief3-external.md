## 1. Buying a dissolved company's home from the Crown (bona vacantia)

**There is a published, free, non-FOI purchase route.** When a company is dissolved its property vests in the Crown as bona vacantia (s.1012 Companies Act 2006), administered by the **Government Legal Department, Bona Vacantia Division (BVD)** for most of England & Wales (the Duchies of Lancaster/Cornwall in their areas; KLTR in Scotland). [secondary] https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/bona-vacantia

**How to buy — the BVC2 referral.** Complete the **"BVC2 Online Referral Form for Land and Buildings"** and email it to `bvcompanies@governmentlegal.gov.uk` with the building description, the interest you want, occupancy, planning consents and mortgage info. [verified: primary] https://www.gov.uk/guidance/refer-bona-vacantia-land-or-buildings-bvc2 (Related published guides: BVC1 overview, BVC4 freehold reversion, BVC6 mortgagee sale, BVC7 shares, BVC8 IP.)

GLD's requirements, quoted from BVC2/BVC1 [verified: primary]:
- **Always open-market value:** *"If BVD sell bona vacantia land it is always for the open market value subject to a minimum price."*
- **Auction preferred:** *"where possible, land will be sold at public auction."*
- **No guarantee:** *"There is no guarantee that they will sell to you, or at all."*
- **Minimum price:** will not sell *"for less than £1,000 plus their costs."*
- **Valuation by the District Valuer**, DV fees **~£2,400–£4,440**, non-refundable, paid by applicant.
- **Buyer's legal costs £998.75 + VAT** + disbursements. **AML ID checks** at ≥£10,000.
- **No title guarantee:** *"BVD do not transfer property with either full or limited title guarantee"* — all risk on the buyer.
- **No fixed timescale**, but urgency matters: *"you will increase the risk of the land being disclaimed if you do not act quickly."* If disclaimed, a Gazette disclaimer notice is published (this is the code-2603 feed) and the asset can escheat to the Crown Estate.

**Company restoration — the alternative, for connected parties only.** Restoration revests the property in the company (s.1032 CA 2006, treated as if never dissolved):
- **Administrative restoration** (form RT01, Companies House): only a **former director/member**; company struck off ≤6 years ago, trading at dissolution, not a voluntary strike-off; fee **£341** + a Bona Vacantia waiver letter if it held assets. [verified: primary] https://www.gov.uk/restore-dissolved-company
- **Court-order restoration:** broad "interested party" list (incl. *"a shared or competing interest in land"*); **£326 court fee** (as read this run; some secondary sources say £318); generally a **6-year** limit (s.1030 CA 2006). [verified: primary for £326] https://www.gov.uk/claiming-money-or-property-from-dissolved-company/restore-company-court-order
- A third-party developer with **no** company connection cannot restore — BVC2 purchase is their only route. BVD normally allows a restoration window, which is why speed matters on the purchase side. [inferred]

**Does GLD publish a list of property it holds/sold?** **No.** Only the Unclaimed Estates List (deceased persons, no addresses, not company land) and individual Gazette disclaimer notices. A buyer must already know the property (Companies House dissolution + HM Land Registry proprietor = the dissolved company), then use BVC2. [verified: primary — no list in BVC1/BVC2]

**What this lets the lane do:** for the 75 company-limbo homes, gives a concrete free path — confirm the proprietor is a dissolved company, submit the free BVC2 referral, and expect open-market/auction pricing (min £1,000 + ~£1k legal + £2.4–4.4k DV, no title guarantee); know a connected party could instead restore the company (£341 admin / £326 court), so move before disclaimer or restoration closes the window.

## 2. Proving "empty 2+ / 10+ years" for reduced/zero-rated VAT

Primary source: **HMRC VAT Notice 708** (read this run). https://www.gov.uk/guidance/buildings-and-construction-vat-notice-708
- **5%** reduced rate: renovation of a dwelling **not lived in during the 2 years** before work starts (s.8). **Zero rate** on the first sale/long lease after renovation: **not lived in during the 10 years** before (s.5). [verified: primary]

**Evidence HMRC accepts (quoted):** *"Proof of such can be obtained from Electoral Roll and Council Tax records, utilities companies, Empty Property Officers in local authorities, or any other source that can be considered reliable."* [verified: primary]

**The Empty Property Officer (EPO) letter is decisive (quoted):** *"If you hold a letter from an Empty Property Officer certifying that the property has not been lived in for [the qualifying period], you do not need any other evidence. If an Empty Property Officer is unsure … he should write with their best estimate. We may then call for other supporting evidence."* [verified: primary]
- So: an **EPO certification letter is a complete answer**; if the council can only best-estimate, corroborate with Council Tax empty records + electoral-roll absence + utility consumption.
- **Land Registry is not on Notice 708's non-occupation list** — it shows ownership, not occupation. Treat as context only. [inferred, against the primary list]

**Can a prospective buyer get the evidence pre-purchase? Genuinely unsettled — per-council policy, not a national rule.** [verified: primary that the notice is silent on applicant identity] The EPO letter attests a fact about the *property*, not a person; some councils confirm empty status to any enquirer about an address, others may want the owner involved. Example primary quote (Cumberland Council): *"A letter from your local empty property officer or council tax records would usually be sufficient evidence."* Do **not** assume a buyer can always obtain it before completion — verify per council. [flagged for adviser]

**Tribunal case law on empty-period evidence sufficiency:** none on-point surfaced this run (BAILII + gov.uk tribunal listings returned other VAT/dwelling points). Nearest is CJEU *HPA – Construções* [2024] C-433/22 (different point). Absence-in-search ≠ none exists; a dedicated FTT-database/paid-service check is needed before relying on "no case law." [flagged]

**What this lets the lane do:** front-load a deal by chasing the EPO certification letter (the single item HMRC accepts without more) for each target, with a Council-Tax + electoral-roll + utilities bundle as fallback — while treating "can a pre-purchase buyer obtain the letter?" as a per-council question to confirm.

## 6. Before anyone writes to an executor (legal groundwork only — not doing outreach now)

Primary sources: ICO B2B-marketing and legitimate-interests pages (read this run). A Gazette s.27 notice names the executor and usually a solicitor/firm; its stated purpose is inviting **creditors' claims**, so reusing it to pitch a purchase is a *different* purpose — a purpose-limitation risk to document.

**Writing to the named solicitors' FIRM — likely lawful with care:**
- PECR email consent rule *"does not apply to corporate subscribers"*; *"If you are intending to send marketing by post then PECR does not apply."* But a **sole practitioner / some partnerships count as individual subscribers**, so email to them needs consent/soft opt-in. [verified: primary]
- UK GDPR still applies to any personal data; where PECR doesn't require consent, *"legitimate interests will be the appropriate lawful basis"* — needing a documented Legitimate Interest Assessment (three-part test) and easy opt-out. Business recipient + a purchase enquiry the PR must field = easier balance. [verified: primary / inferred]
- **Net:** likely lawful by **post (or B2B email to a corporate/LLP firm)** with an LIA on file; higher care where the "solicitor" is a sole-trader/partnership individual subscriber.

**Writing to a lay/family EXECUTOR — high-risk / likely inadvisable:**
- An identified individual = individual subscriber → unsolicited **email/text effectively barred** (no consent/soft opt-in on a cold approach). Post is outside PECR but UK GDPR's balancing is materially harder — ICO weighs *"the potential nuisance factor"* and *"the effect … on more vulnerable individuals,"* and *"if someone objects … this overrides your legitimate interests."* Recently bereaved recipient + death-sourced data = reasonable-expectation and vulnerability factors weigh against it. [verified: primary / inferred]

**SRA angle (supports routing through the solicitor):** a PR owes a best-price duty and must avoid conflicts; a **credible, identified purchase enquiry routed through the named firm is not inherently unwelcome** — it can be logged as an offer the PR must weigh. [secondary — SRA probate thematic review / conflict guidance]

**No ICO enforcement decision specifically on Gazette-s.27 reuse was found this run** (not found ≠ none exists). All of this is for a solicitor to confirm, including the individual-vs-corporate-subscriber call.

**What this lets the lane do:** if outreach is ever switched on, route Gazette-sourced approaches through the **named firm** by post/B2B email (documented LIA, easy opt-out), treat cold contact of a lay executor as effectively off-limits, and frame every approach as a serious purchase enquiry the PR can weigh against their best-price duty — with a solicitor signing it off.

## 8. CPO and EDMO as a source — and correcting the "~182 register"

**The register the earlier note cited is real and correctly identified — it just isn't an empty-homes-only register.** "**Compulsory purchase orders: register of decisions**" (gov.uk): a spreadsheet of *"decisions on all housing, planning and regeneration compulsory purchase orders in England which were submitted to the Secretary of State,"* first published Mar 2024, updated ~monthly (latest 28 Jul 2026). [verified: primary] https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/compulsory-purchase-orders-register-of-decisions
- The earlier **~178–182 "empty individual houses since 2019" (Burnley/Bradford leading)** was a **derived count** — empty-dwelling CPOs filtered out of this all-CPO register by reading order titles. It stands as a filtered count, **not** an official "empty-homes CPO register." Caveat: it only captures CPOs that reached a **Secretary-of-State decision** (submitted + confirmed/refused), not CPOs still in progress or confirmed without SoS involvement. [inferred] *(An initial research pass this run mistook this for a "phantom" register; the register exists — the correction is only that it's an all-CPO decisions list you filter, not an empty-homes list.)*

**Pulling CPOs from The Gazette (mirrors the 2903/2603 feeds):** CPOs have no dedicated code; they gazette under the enabling power. Primary code **1601 "Town and country planning"** (a `noticetypes=1601` feed filtered on CPO text returned 245 results, with a confirmed worked example under s.226 TCPA 1990 + Acquisition of Land Act 1981); **1503 "Highways"** for highway CPOs; retired codes 1602/1603/1604 hold older housing CPOs. [verified: primary]
`https://www.thegazette.co.uk/all-notices/notice/data.feed?noticetypes=1601` (add `&text=compulsory+purchase` + a council/area term to localise).

**EDMOs (Empty Dwelling Management Orders):** Housing Act 2004 Pt 4 Ch 2; a **local housing authority** takes over *management* (not ownership) of a dwelling **wholly unoccupied ≥2 years**; interim order must be authorised by the **First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber)**. **Very rarely used, and no free national register** — only scattered tribunal decisions and council committee papers; **no Gazette code**. Not a scalable data source. [secondary / verified: primary that the code list has no EDMO entry]

**What this lets the lane do:** keep the CPO **register of decisions** as a real (if all-CPO, SoS-decided-only) address source, and *additionally* pull live CPOs from The Gazette via `noticetypes=1601` + text/area filters the same way as deceased-estates; treat EDMOs as a rare tribunal-driven signal with no free feed.
