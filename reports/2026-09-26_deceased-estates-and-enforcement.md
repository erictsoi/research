# PART A — Council tax and deceased estates

## 1. Class F exemption

### 1.1 Legal basis
- The power to prescribe exempt dwellings is in **Local Government Finance Act 1992 (LGFA) s.4**. The exemption is **Class F in article 3 of the Council Tax (Exempt Dwellings) Order 1992, SI 1992/558**. It was amended by SI 1993/150 and by **SI 1994/539 art 4**, which extended it to deceased tenants. — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/558/article/3 ; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1994/539/article/4/made **[secondary: extract of primary]** (the amendment history is **[inferred]**)
- **I could not get the current verbatim text of Class F.** A search extract of the *as made* (1992) version reads: "an unoccupied dwelling in relation to which a person is a qualifying person in his capacity as personal representative, if either no grant of probate or of letters of administration has been made, or less than 6 months have elapsed since the day on which such a grant was made." — https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/558/article/3/made **[secondary: extract of primary; may be a search-index paraphrase, and it is not the current text]**
- The current elements, as described by secondary sources:
  1. the dwelling is **unoccupied** **[secondary]**;
  2. the only person who would otherwise be liable would be liable **only in their capacity as personal representative (PR)** of the deceased — Commons Library CBP-10651, https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10651/ **[secondary]**;
  3. the deceased held a freehold or a lease of 6 months or more, **or** was a tenant whose PR is liable for rent or a licence fee (the 1994 extension) — https://www.malvernhills.gov.uk/council-tax/council-tax-when-someone-passes-away **[secondary]**;
  4. the time limit described in 1.2.
- **Flag:** it is unconfirmed whether the current text also requires the dwelling to have been unoccupied **continuously since the death**. The Commons Library says Class F is not available where the property does not become unoccupied at death (for example, a surviving spouse stays on) **[secondary]**.

### 1.2 Duration
- **England:** exempt from the date of death until a grant of probate or letters of administration, **with no time limit before the grant**, and then for **up to 6 months after the date of the grant**, as long as the conditions continue to be met. — Commons Library CBP-10651; B&NES, https://www.bathnes.gov.uk/apply-council-tax-probate-exemption **[secondary]**
- **A long delay in applying for probate does not end Class F in England.** In *Tew v Lewisham LBC* (VTE, 2018) the council withdrew Class F because of a long delay in applying for probate and served backdated demands of more than £14,000; the tribunal ordered the exemption reinstated. *NR v Maldon DC* (VTE, 2019) followed it. — Alan Murdie, "Taxing Decisions", https://medium.com/@chauntelle.wright/taxing-decisions-by-alan-murdie-addd84ca797e **[secondary; I could not read the decisions themselves, and VTE decisions are not binding]**

### 1.3 Who can claim
- In practice the **PR** makes the claim: an **executor** (named in the will) or an **administrator** (appointed by letters of administration). Councils ask for the death certificate, the PR's details, the will or a solicitor's letter, and later a copy of the grant, plus the date the property was cleared and the date of sale or transfer. — https://www.bathnes.gov.uk/apply-council-tax-probate-exemption ; https://www.afterloss.uk/guides/council-tax-after-death **[secondary]**
- **Why the PR is liable in the first place:** under LGFA 1992 s.6, where nobody is resident, the **owner** is liable. s.6(5) defines "owner" by reference to a **material interest**, meaning a freehold or a lease granted for 6 months or more. — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/14/section/6 **[secondary: extract of primary]**. On death, the estate vests in the executor (Administration of Estates Act 1925 s.1). On intestacy it vests in the Public Trustee until letters are granted (AEA 1925 s.9, as amended in 1994). **[inferred]**
- **The deceased's own account:** under **SI 1992/613 reg 58**, the executor or administrator is liable for the deceased's unpaid council tax up to the deceased's liability, and is **entitled to any excess the deceased paid**. — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/613/regulation/58/made **[secondary: extract of primary, made version]**

### 1.4 What ends it
Sources: Commons Library; councils; Greenhalgh Kerr, https://www.greenhalghkerr.com/articles/council-tax-liability-on-death/ **[secondary]**
- **6 months pass after the grant.**
- **Someone occupies** the dwelling.
- **Someone becomes liable in a different capacity.** Examples are a sale completing, or an **assent or transfer to a beneficiary**: "as soon as the property is sold or assented to a beneficiary, someone is liable in a different capacity and the exemption stops".
- **Deceased tenant:** the tenancy ends or is re-let, so the PR is no longer liable for rent.
- **Surviving joint owner:** Class F does not apply, because the survivor is liable as owner in their own right. In LGSCO decision **20 011 625**, a council had wrongly granted Class F to a son who was joint owner with his late mother. — https://www.lgo.org.uk/decisions/benefits-and-tax/council-tax/20-011-625 **[secondary]**
- **Class E → Class F:** if the deceased had moved into hospital or a care home, Class E (which requires the person to be *living* elsewhere) stops at death, and Class F can start from the date of death if the PR is the only person liable **[inferred]**.

### 1.5 After Class F ends (England)
- **Standard charge:** councils have discretion over empty-home discounts (SI 2003/3011), and many set 0%, so the **full charge** usually applies **[secondary / inferred]**.
- **Long-term empty premium:** **LGFA 1992 s.11B**, as amended by **Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023 s.79**. From financial years beginning **1 April 2024**, it applies after **1 year** empty and substantially unfurnished (previously 2 years). — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/55/section/79 **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- **The probate exception is "Class I"** of the Council Tax (Prescribed Classes of Dwellings) (England) Regulations 2003 (**SI 2003/3011**), inserted by **SI 2024/1007**. It covers a dwelling that was in exemption Class F and for which a grant has been made, until **12 months after the grant**. It ends early on completion of a **"relevant transaction"**. — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1007/made **[secondary: extract of primary]**
  - **⚠ Correction to brief:** the brief dates this exception from 1 April 2024. **SI 2024/1007 came into force on 1 November 2024, and the exceptions apply from 1 April 2025** (Council Tax information letter 5/2024). The 1 April 2024 date belongs to the s.79 cut to the premium threshold. — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/council-tax-information-letter-52024-exceptions-to-council-tax-premiums **[secondary: extract of primary]**
  - **Name clash:** premium exception "Class F" is for annexes, not deceased persons. Always write "exemption Class F" and "premium exception Class I". — https://modgov.north-norfolk.gov.uk/documents/s22088/Appendix%20C%20-%20The%20Council%20Tax%20Premium%20Exceptions.pdf **[secondary]**
  - **Practical sequence:** exemption Class F runs from death to 6 months after the grant. Then the full charge applies with no premium until 12 months after the grant (Class I). Then Class G or H (actively marketed for sale or let, 12 months) may apply. **[secondary + inferred]**
  - **Flag:** it is unconfirmed whether an **assent to a beneficiary** counts as a "relevant transaction" that ends Class I.

### 1.6 Wales — different and recently changed
- **The Council Tax (Discounts, Disregards and Exemptions) (Wales) Regulations 2026, WSI 2026/8.** In Wales, Class F applies where **(a) no grant has been made and the second anniversary of the death has not passed**, or **(b) the grant was made less than 12 months ago**. So Wales has a **2-year cap before the grant** (England has none) and a **12-month period after the grant** (England: 6 months). — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/8/body/made ; https://www.gov.wales/written-statement-council-tax-discounts-disregards-and-exemptions-consultation-summary-responses **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- **Flag — commencement uncertain:** one extract says in force 1 April 2026. Amending regulations laid on **25 September 2026** make "minor changes to Classes A, C and F … clarifying provisions relating to probate exemptions", with transitional provisions referring to 1 April 2027 and 1 April 2028. — https://www.gov.wales/written-statement-council-tax-discounts-disregards-and-exemptions-wales-amendment-regulations-2026 **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- **Welsh premium exceptions:** WSI 2015/2068 sets out Classes 1–7. **There is no probate exception**, and the Commons Library confirms that Class I does not apply in Wales. **I found no Welsh "Class 11/12" as the brief suggested.** — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2015/2068/made **[secondary: extract of primary]**

### 1.7 How councils handle claims in practice
- **Tell Us Once** passes the death to the council (Council Tax Reduction, Housing Benefit, Blue Badge, electoral register and similar). Secondary sources agree it is **not a Class F claim and does not settle the account**; the PR still has to contact the council tax team. — https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death/organisations-you-need-to-contact-and-tell-us-once **[secondary: extract of primary]**; https://www.bradford.gov.uk/council-tax/tell-us-about-a-change-of-address-and-other-changes/council-tax-tell-us-a-resident-or-owner-has-died/ **[secondary]**. **Flag:** some councils (for example Newcastle) tell residents they need not contact them separately after Tell Us Once.
- Example council pages **[secondary]**:
  - B&NES: https://www.bathnes.gov.uk/apply-council-tax-probate-exemption
  - Malvern Hills: https://www.malvernhills.gov.uk/council-tax/council-tax-when-someone-passes-away
  - Royal Greenwich: https://www.royalgreenwich.gov.uk/council-tax/tell-us-youre-moving-or-about-change/tell-us-someone-has-died/if-property-now-empty
  - North West Leicestershire: https://www.nwleics.gov.uk/pages/class_f_exemption_deceased
  - Welsh forms: Conwy https://www.conwy.gov.uk/en/Resident/Council-Tax/Discounts-reductions-exemptions/Assets-Exemptions/assets/documents/Application-form-exempt-dwellings-Class-F.pdf
- **Practice varies:** some councils have an online form, others want a letter. Evidence is typically the death certificate and later the grant.

## 2. Refunds where the estate paid during a Class F period

### 2.1 The legal basis for a refund
- **The exemption does not depend on a claim.** An exempt dwelling is not a chargeable dwelling (LGFA 1992 s.4), and Class F contains no application requirement as far as the secondary sources show. So tax paid for days that met Class F was **not legally due**. **[inferred — check the verbatim text]**
- **The council has duties:** under **SI 1992/613 reg 8** the council must take "reasonable steps to ascertain whether any dwellings in its area will be or were exempt dwellings". Under **reg 9**, where it has reason to believe a dwelling was exempt, it must assume so. — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/613/made **[secondary: extract of primary]**
  - **⚠ Correction to brief:** regs 14–15 are the equivalent *discount* provisions; exemptions are **regs 8–9**.
  - **[inferred]** Once a council knows of a death (for example through Tell Us Once) and that the property is empty, it arguably "has reason to believe" Class F applies.
- **Repayment:** under **SI 1992/613 reg 31**, an overpayment "shall be repaid if the liable person so requires", or else is repaid or credited. — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/613/regulation/31/made/data.htm **[secondary: extract of primary; the extract may have mixed in reg 24 wording]**

### 2.2 Routes
1. **Ask the council** to apply Class F from the date of death and repay under reg 31. Secondary sources say exemption claims have no time limit and are backdated once approved. — https://www.bcpcouncil.gov.uk/council-tax/council-tax-discounts-and-exemptions/appeal-your-council-tax-liability-discount-or-exemption **[secondary]**
2. **Appeal under LGFA 1992 s.16:**
   - First serve written notice on the council under **s.16(4)**. It then has **2 months** to reply (s.16(7)).
   - Then appeal to the Valuation Tribunal for England (VTE). Under **SI 2009/2269 reg 21**, the appeal is due within **2 months** of the council's decision, or within **4 months** of the s.16(4) notice if the council has not replied. The VTE President can extend time.
   - Sources: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/14/section/16 **[secondary: extract of primary]**; https://valuationtribunal.gov.uk/council-tax-appeals/council-tax-liability-appeal/ ; https://england.shelter.org.uk/professional_resources/legal/debt/council_tax/liability_appeals **[secondary]**
   - **There is no statutory time limit on the initial s.16(4) notice** (Shelter) **[secondary]**.
3. **Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman** for maladministration, although it usually declines where a VTE appeal is available **[secondary / inferred]**.
4. **Discretionary reduction** under LGFA 1992 s.13A(1)(c), as a fallback **[inferred]**.
5. **Restitution claim** in court (*Woolwich Equitable BS v IRC* [1993] AC 70) **[inferred]**.

### 2.3 How far back? — UNRESOLVED
- **No express backdating limit** appears in LGFA 1992, SI 1992/558 or SI 1992/613, as far as the extracts show **[inferred]**.
- **Limitation Act 1980** could apply to a *court* claim **[inferred; Act not read]**:
  - **s.9:** 6 years for sums recoverable under an enactment.
  - **s.5:** 6 years for contract and, by analogy, restitution.
  - **s.32(1)(c):** for mistake, time runs from discovery; see *FII Group Litigation v HMRC* [2020] UKSC 47.
- **Tension:** a s.16 grievance has no initial time limit, and a VTE direction can require recalculation for any period (as in *Tew*). It is **unclear whether a council can apply a 6-year limit to repay under reg 31**. Councils recovering arrears are limited to 6 years by **SI 1992/613 reg 34(3)** (https://england.shelter.org.uk/professional_resources/debt_advice/resources_for_debt_advisers/council_tax_debt_more_than_six_years_old **[secondary]**), and some councils reportedly apply a 6-year practice to refunds by analogy **[secondary, low reliability]**. **This needs a solicitor's view.**

## 3. Band challenges connected to a death

### 3.1 Legislation
- **England:** the Council Tax (Alteration of Lists and Appeals) (England) Regulations 2009, **SI 2009/2270**, made under LGFA 1992 s.24. — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/2270/made **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- **Wales — ⚠ Correction to brief:** there is no stand-alone 2005 Welsh set. Wales uses the **1993 Regulations (SI 1993/290)**, as amended by **WSI 2005/181** and WSI 2010/77. Appeals go to the Valuation Tribunal for Wales. — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2005/181/made **[secondary: extract of primary]**

### 3.2 Can an executor or PR make a proposal?
- Under **reg 2**, "interested person" includes the **owner**, and any person who would be liable if the dwelling were not exempt. **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- **Probably yes [inferred]:** the PR is the s.6(5) owner once the estate vests, and during Class F is the person who would be liable but for the exemption.
- **Flag:** the 6-month "new taxpayer" route may not fit a PR neatly. During Class F nobody is liable, so a PR may only "become the taxpayer" when Class F ends. Other grounds, and the VOA's own power to alter, remain available. **[inferred]**

### 3.3 Can a new owner after a probate sale?
- **Yes.** Under **reg 4**, a person who becomes the taxpayer, and has not previously been the taxpayer for that dwelling during the list, may make a proposal **within 6 months of becoming the taxpayer**. — VOA blog, https://valuationoffice.blog.gov.uk/2023/02/03/council-tax-band-challenges/ **[secondary]**; reg text **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- **⚠ Correction to brief:** the circumstances for proposals, including the time limits, appear in **reg 4**. **Reg 3** appears to cover the listing officer's own alterations. **[secondary: extract of primary — check]**
- Other reg 4 grounds include a **material reduction** in value, for example demolition of part of the dwelling, a change in the locality, or adaptation for a disabled person, and a VTE or High Court decision on a comparable dwelling. **[secondary: extract of primary / inferred]**

### 3.4 How far back a reduction is effective (reg 11)
- A **material reduction** takes effect from the day the circumstances arose. **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- A correction of an inaccuracy **in the list as compiled** that lowers the band is **backdated**: in effect to **1 April 1993**, or to the date the dwelling entered the list. **Increases are not backdated.** — reg 11 **[secondary: extract of primary, partly paraphrased]**. MSE describes refunds "to when you moved into the property or 1993 (whichever is later)": https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/reclaim/council-tax-bands-change/ **[secondary]**
- **Estate angle [inferred, UNCERTAIN]:** because a compiled-list correction applies to the dwelling from 1993, the **deceased's own years** may also have been overcharged. The PR may be entitled to the excess under reg 58 and reg 31. Whether councils refund earlier taxpayers, and subject to what limit, is unclear.
- **HMRC IHTM10161:** HMRC treats the right to apply for rebanding as **an estate asset at the date of death**, and may accept a deduction for an agent's charge of up to 50% of the refund. — https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10161 **[secondary: extract of primary]**. This implies HMRC expects estates to receive band refunds.

### 3.5 Current VOA process
- There are **two routes**, both through gov.uk's "Challenge your Council Tax band" service — https://www.gov.uk/challenge-council-tax-band ; VOA blog https://valuationoffice.blog.gov.uk/2024/11/12/evidence-to-support-your-council-tax-band-challenge/ **[secondary]**:
  1. **Formal proposal:** needs a statutory ground, for example being a new taxpayer within 6 months. The VOA must decide it, and there is a right of appeal to the VTE.
  2. **Band review:** informal, needs strong evidence, can take up to 12 months, and has **no right of appeal**.
- **[inferred]** A successful review is implemented by the listing officer's own alteration, so reg 11 backdating still applies.
- **Flag:** the VOA appears to have been merged into HMRC (the blog is now titled "HMRC Valuation Office"); the date is unconfirmed **[secondary / inferred]**.

## 9. Regulation: band and discount checking vs FCA-regulated debt advice

- **⚠ Correction to brief — article numbers.** In the Regulated Activities Order (SI 2001/544):
  - **art 39D** is debt adjusting;
  - **art 39E** is debt counselling;
  - **art 39F** is debt collecting;
  - **art 39G** is debt administration.

  Sources: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/39E ; https://service.betterregulation.com/document/147369 **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- **Debt counselling (art 39E)** means advice to a borrower "about the liquidation of a debt due under a **credit agreement**", or to a hirer about a consumer hire agreement. **Debt adjusting (art 39D)** means negotiating with the **lender or owner** over such debts. **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- **Council tax is a statutory liability, not a credit agreement.** So **checking a band, identifying discounts or exemptions, helping claim CTR, lodging a band challenge or s.16 appeal, and (probably) negotiating council tax arrears with the council are outside arts 39D–39E**. **[inferred — strong, but confirm with a regulatory lawyer]**
- **Where the line is crossed:** PERG 17.3 and 17.4 say debt counselling is not limited to cases where all the debts are credit debts. Advice covering both kinds "is likely to be debt counselling as what is done about non-consumer credit debt is likely to affect consumer credit debt, particularly if the advice does not distinguish between the two types of debt". — https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg17/perg17s4 **[secondary: extract of primary]**. **[inferred]** Any advice about **which debts to prioritise** is likely regulated once any credit debt is involved; for example, "pay council tax first and cut your card payments". Budgeting across debts, or recommending a DMP or DRO, is too. Council tax is a priority debt, so arrears conversations drift towards this naturally. **Design rule: stop at the council tax facts and refer on.**
- **Breathing Space** can be started only by a **debt advice provider**: an FCA-authorised art 39E firm or a local authority (SI 2020/1311, reg 3 per extract). An unregulated service must refer. — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/1311/part/1/made **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- **Claims management:** the regulated kinds of claim are personal injury, financial services or products, housing disrepair, "specified benefit" (industrial injuries-type only), criminal injury and employment. **Council tax refunds, appeals and band challenges do not appear to be regulated claims.** **[secondary + inferred; check the RAO Part 3B text and PERG 2.7]**
- **Legal Services Act 2007:** the reserved "probate activities" (Sch 2 para 6) cover preparing the papers for a grant. Estate administration, including council tax work, is **not reserved**. — https://legalservicesboard.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Guidance-on-probate-services.pdf **[secondary]**
- **VTE representation:** SI 2009/2269 reportedly lets a party appoint a representative "whether a legal representative or not". The regulation number is unconfirmed. **[secondary: extract of primary]**
- **Referral fees:** the FCA banned **debt packagers** from receiving referral fees from debt solution providers (CONC 8; rule number and date unconfirmed). — https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/financial-watchdog-bans-referral-fees-debt-packagers-help-struggling-consumers **[secondary]**

# Summaries

**Part A — Deceased estates.**
- **The law is generous but the process is manual.** In England, an empty home held by a PR is exempt from council tax under Class F (SI 1992/558 art 3) from death until the grant, with no time limit, and for 6 months after it. A further 12-month exception from the empty-homes premium (Class I, SI 2003/3011 as amended by SI 2024/1007) has applied from 1 April 2025.
- **Wales has just diverged:** a 2-year cap before the grant and 12 months after it (WSI 2026/8), with commencement uncertain.
- **Nothing is triggered automatically.** Tell Us Once notifies the council, but it does not apply the exemption or settle the account.
- **Refunds can reach well into the past.** Exemption is not claim-dependent. Councils must take reasonable steps to identify exempt dwellings (SI 1992/613 regs 8–9) and repay overpayments (reg 31). The first s.16 complaint has no time limit. Whether a 6-year limitation cap applies is unresolved.
- **Band challenges add a second recovery route.** A PR is probably an "interested person". A buyer after a probate sale can make a proposal within 6 months (SI 2009/2270 reg 4). Compiled-list reductions backdate to 1993 (reg 11), so the deceased's own years may be refundable, and HMRC already treats that right as an estate asset.
- **Market size:**
  - about 571k deaths a year;
  - an estimated ~190–210k owner-occupied homes left without an occupant (inferred);
  - about 295k grants a year;
  - about 41–46% of probate applications come from lay executors.
- **No service was found that specifically recovers council tax for estates.** That finding needs further checking.

**Part B — Enforcement.**
- **The scale is large and rising.** England's council tax arrears reached a record **£7.4bn** at 31 March 2026, and in-year collection fell to 95.6%. Charity FOI data shows about **1.7m** council tax cases a year passed to bailiffs. About **2.4m** liability orders a year is a widely cited but unsourced estimate.
- **The process escalates fast.** One missed instalment can make the whole year due in about 14 days. Court costs are typically £70–£130+, and bailiff fees rose on 1 May 2026.
- **Band and discount errors are shut out of court.** They cannot be argued at the liability order hearing (reg 57), and must go through the council, the VOA and the VTE in parallel. That makes early checking valuable.
- **Reforms:** England has announced a 63-day grace period, a £100 costs cap and 12 instalments by default, from April 2027, not yet in force. Wales already has the 63-day rule.
- **Unclaimed support is large:** about £3.3bn of Council Tax Support across Great Britain and £1.7bn of pensioner CTR in England.
- **Regulation:** checking bands, discounts and exemptions appears to fall outside FCA debt counselling (RAO art 39E), because council tax is not a credit debt. Advice on prioritising debts where any credit debt exists crosses the line.
- **Referrals:** StepChange, National Debtline and the MaPS Money Adviser Network all run open referral routes, which a checking service could use for onward referral.

# Five facts most useful to a grant application (social impact)

All five must be checked against the primary source before submission.

1. **Record arrears:** England's council tax arrears reached **£7.4bn at 31 March 2026**, up 11.3% in a year, with in-year collection down to **95.6%** (MHCLG 2025-26). **[secondary: extract of primary]**
2. **Bailiff exposure:** about **1.72 million** council tax cases were passed to bailiffs in 2025-26 across England and Wales, and only 26 councils exempt Council Tax Support recipients (Stop the Knock 2026 FOI). **[secondary]**
3. **Unclaimed help:** **£3.3bn** of Council Tax Support goes unclaimed each year in Great Britain (Policy in Practice 2025). Pensioners in England alone miss **£1.7bn** of CTR, with about 50% take-up (Independent Age). **[secondary]**
4. **Errors are locked out of court:** a wrong band or a missed discount or exemption **cannot be raised at the liability order hearing** (SI 1992/613 reg 57). It must be caught through the council, the VOA or the VTE, ideally before enforcement costs are added. From 1 May 2026, bailiff fees alone start at £79 and rise to £247 at the enforcement stage (SI 2026/366). **[secondary: extract of primary]**
5. **Bereaved households:** about **571,000** people died in England and Wales in 2025. An estimated **~190,000–210,000** owner-occupied homes a year pass to estates with no one living in them **[inferred]**. Exemption from death to 6 months after grant is not applied automatically, and **about 100,000+ lay executors a year** (41–46% of probate applications) handle this without professional help (HMCTS FCSQ). **[secondary: extract of primary / inferred]**

# Open legal questions for a solicitor

1. **Class F wording:** what is the exact current text of SI 1992/558 art 3 Class F? Must the dwelling have been unoccupied continuously since death? How does it apply to deceased tenants and part-owners?
2. **Refund limitation:** after a retrospective Class F award, or a successful s.16 appeal, can a council limit repayment under SI 1992/613 reg 31 to 6 years (Limitation Act 1980 ss.5, 9)? Does s.32(1)(c) (mistake) extend it? How does this interact with the absence of a time limit on s.16(4) notices?
3. **Council duty:** once a council receives a Tell Us Once notification and knows the property is empty, does SI 1992/613 regs 8–9 oblige it to apply Class F without a claim? What remedy follows if it keeps billing?
4. **Class I:** does an **assent to a beneficiary** count as a "relevant transaction" that ends premium exception Class I (SI 2003/3011 as amended by SI 2024/1007)?
5. **PR band proposals:** is a PR an "interested person" under SI 2009/2270 reg 2? When does a PR "become the taxpayer" for the reg 4 6-month window if the dwelling was exempt at first? Does a PR who later takes as beneficiary count as the same person?
6. **Backdated band refunds for the deceased's years:** after a compiled-list reduction backdated under reg 11, can the PR claim the deceased's overpayments (reg 58 and reg 31), and how far back?
7. **Wales:** when does WSI 2026/8 Class F commence, what do the amending regulations laid on 25 September 2026 change, and what transitional rules apply to deaths before commencement?
8. **FCA perimeter:** confirm that checking bands, discounts and exemptions, and negotiating council tax arrears with a council, are outside RAO arts 39D–39G. Where exactly does a script have to stop when the client also has credit debts (PERG 17.3–17.4)?
9. **Claims management and consumer law:** confirm that council tax refund and band work is not a regulated claims management activity. Is a contingency fee on refunds from an estate fair and enforceable under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 and the DMCC Act 2024? What fee level is defensible given MSE's 25% benchmark and HMRC's 50% IHT allowance?
10. **Authority and capacity:** what written authority do councils, the VOA and the VTE accept from a third-party agent acting for an executor or a living taxpayer? For SMI and cognitive-impairment claims, when is an LPA, deputyship or appointeeship required (Mental Capacity Act 2005)? What UK GDPR Art 9 condition covers the health data?
11. **Liability orders:** where an exemption or discount is later established on s.16 appeal, how is an existing liability order varied or set aside (*Newham v Stratford MC*)? Are summons costs refundable?
12. **Implementation dates:** confirm the England reforms of 15 April 2026 (63 days, £100 cap, 12 instalments by default, SMI renamed) and their implementing SIs, and the exact SI 2026/366 fee schedule.
