**Material corrections in revision 2** (each is explained in its section):

1. **England Class F wording.** The dwelling must have been **unoccupied since the date of death**, disregarding one occupation of up to 6 weeks (art 2(3)).
   - The 6-month post-grant limit does not apply to limb (2)(a)(i), where no one is a qualifying person.
2. **An assent to a beneficiary does *not* end premium exception Class I.** A "relevant transaction" is a "transfer on sale". The assent does still end *exemption* Class F.
3. **Wales.** WSI 2026/8 came into force on **1 April 2026**.
   - Its Class F text was defective. WSI 2026/118 restores the "unoccupied since death" condition from **30 October 2026**.
   - Deaths before 1 April 2026 are treated as occurring on 1 April 2026. The 2027/2028 dates in revision 1 relate to Class H, not Class F.
4. **Band challenges (SI 2009/2270).**
   - Reg 3 lists the grounds for *any* alteration.
   - Under reg 11(7)(b), correcting a compiled-list error takes effect from the day the list was compiled, which is 1 April 1993 in England. "Or the date the dwelling entered the list" was wrong.
   - A reg 4(3) proposal that the compiled band was wrong is only available in the first 6 months of the list or within 6 months of becoming a new taxpayer.
5. **HMRC IHTM10161.** The 50% is a *valuation discount* for uncertainty and delay. It is not HMRC accepting agent fees of up to 50%.
6. **Time from death to probate application now has an official source.** HMCTS says about 70% of applications are made within 6 months of death.
7. **England arrears.** The increase is **+£782m (+11.8%)**, per the revised release of 31 July 2026, not +£748m.
   - The MHCLG release contains **no liability order counts**.
   - The "2.4m liability orders" figure is **unsupported** and has been dropped.
8. **Enforcement steps.**
   - In England no final notice is needed after the reminder route (reg 33(3)).
   - Breathing Space eligibility is triggered by **service of a reminder notice** (SI 2020/1311 reg 5(4)(k)).
   - *Nicolson* and *Newham* are restated using the courts' own tests.
9. **Figures.**
   - PERG quote relocated to PERG 17.3 Q3.3.
   - Disabled reduction for Band A is 5/9 of Band D.
   - Crisis and Resilience Fund England allocation is £858.0m (2026-27 determination).
   - Octopus Legacy grant-only is £585. Fraser & Fraser charges 25% + VAT. Life Ledger is free to families. Kings Court Trust is controlled by Smedvig Capital.
10. **The VOA became part of HMRC on 1 April 2026.**

# PART A — Council tax and deceased estates

## 1. Class F exemption

### 1.1 Legal basis

**The power.** LGFA 1992 s.4 gives the power to prescribe exempt dwellings. **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/14/section/4
- s.4(1): "Council tax shall be payable in respect of any dwelling which is not an exempt dwelling."
- s.4(2): "'exempt dwelling' means any dwelling of a class prescribed by an order made by the Secretary of State."

**The instrument.** Class F is in **article 3 of the Council Tax (Exempt Dwellings) Order 1992, SI 1992/558**. The current Class F was substituted from 1 April 1994 by **SI 1994/539 art 4(d)**, which added deceased tenants; before that, SI 1993/150 art 4(d) had substituted it. **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/558/article/3 ; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1994/539/article/4/made
- legislation.gov.uk banner: "There are currently no known outstanding effects".
- Since 1 April 2026 art 3 applies **in England only**. Note F32 on legislation.gov.uk says Wales moved to WSI 2026/8.

**Current English text, verbatim** **[verified: primary]**:

> Class F:(1) an unoccupied dwelling—
> (a) which has been unoccupied since the date of death of a person ("the deceased"); and
> (b) in relation to which one of the conditions set out in paragraph (2) below is satisfied;
> (2) the conditions referred to in paragraph (1) above are, subject to paragraph (3) below, that—
> (a) the deceased had, at the date of his death, a freehold interest in the dwelling, or a leasehold interest in the dwelling which was granted for a term of six months or more, and
> (i) no person [is] a qualifying person in respect of the dwelling; or
> (ii) a person is a qualifying person in respect of the dwelling acting in his capacity as executor or administrator, and no person is a qualifying person in any other capacity; or
> (b) the deceased was a tenant of the dwelling at the date of his death, and an executor or administrator acting in his capacity as such is liable for rent or, as the case may be, a licence fee, for the day;
> (3) sub-paragraph (a)(ii) and (b) of paragraph (2) above shall only apply, in a case where a grant of probate or letters of administration has been made, if less than six months have elapsed since the date of the grant;

(The word "is" in (2)(a)(i) is missing from the legislation.gov.uk text, which appears to be a transcription slip.)

**Definitions in art 2** **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/558/article/2
- "qualifying person" means a person who would, but for the Order, be liable "as the owner, whether or not jointly with any other person".
- An "unoccupied dwelling" is "a dwelling in which no one lives".
- **Art 2(3):** "For the purposes of Classes B and F … any one period, not exceeding six weeks, during which it was occupied shall be disregarded."

**What this means** **[verified: primary text; application inferred]**:
- The home must have been empty **since the death**, allowing for one stay of up to 6 weeks.
- If a spouse or anyone else stays on for more than 6 weeks, Class F is not available.
- **Limb (2)(a)(i)** covers the case where **no one** is a qualifying person. An example is intestacy before a grant, when the estate vests in the Public Trustee without imposing "any duty, obligation or liability" (AEA 1925 s.9(1), (3)). https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo5/15-16/23/section/9 **[verified: primary]**
- **The 6-month post-grant limit does not apply to limb (2)(a)(i)**, because para (3) names only (a)(ii) and (b).

### 1.2 Duration (England)

- **Before the grant:** no time limit. Para (3) applies only "in a case where a grant … has been made". **[verified: primary]**
- **After the grant:** limbs (a)(ii) and (b) continue until 6 months after the date of the grant. **[verified: primary]**
- MHCLG guidance says the same: "exempt … for as long as it remains unoccupied and until probate is granted. Following a grant … a further 6 months exemption is possible". https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/long-term-empty-homes-and-second-homes-council-tax-premiums-and-exceptions/guidance-on-the-implementation-of-the-council-tax-premiums-on-long-term-empty-homes-and-second-homes **[verified: primary]**
- **Delay in applying for probate:** *Tew v Lewisham LBC* (VTE, 2018) and *NR v Maldon DC* (VTE, 2019) reportedly reinstated Class F after councils withdrew it for delay. **[secondary; blocked: medium.com 403]** The VTE's online search only holds the last 6 years of decisions, so I could not retrieve them. The underlying rule (no pre-grant limit) is **[verified: primary]** from para (3).

### 1.3 Who claims, and liability

- **No application requirement.** Class F contains none. Art 3 applies "if on that day [the dwelling] falls within one of the following classes". **[verified: primary]**
- **Council practice.** Councils ask the personal representative (PR) to notify them. For example B&NES asks you to "register the death with the council", and says "it is important to tell us when the property becomes empty of belongings and furniture". It also asks to be told of the grant, sale or transfer. https://www.bathnes.gov.uk/apply-council-tax-probate-exemption **[secondary]**
- **Evidence for refunds.** Bradford asks for the grant of probate, letters of administration or a copy of the will. https://www.bradford.gov.uk/council-tax/tell-us-about-a-change-of-address-and-other-changes/council-tax-tell-us-a-resident-or-owner-has-died/ **[secondary]**
- **Liability when nobody lives there.** Under LGFA 1992 s.6(2)(f) the owner is liable. **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/14/section/6
  - s.6(5) defines "owner" as the person with a "material interest" not subject to an inferior one.
  - **s.6(6)** defines "material interest" as "a freehold interest, a leasehold interest which was granted for a term of six months or more or a tenancy that is or was previously an assured tenancy". The assured-tenancy words were added **1 May 2026** for specified purposes by the Renters' Rights Act 2025 s.28 and SI 2026/421.
- **The deceased's own account: SI 1992/613 reg 58.** **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/613/regulation/58
  - The PR is liable for the deceased's unpaid tax only "to the extent that it is not in excess of the deceased's liability" (reg 58(2)).
  - Reg 58(4): "Where before the deceased's death a sum in excess of his liability … has been paid … and has not been repaid or credited under Part V, his executor or administrator shall be entitled to the sum."
  - Reg 58(8): the PR "may institute, continue or withdraw proceedings (whether by way of appeal under section 16 of the Act or otherwise)".

### 1.4 What ends it (England)

- **6 months after the grant**, for limbs (a)(ii) and (b). **[verified: primary]**
- **Occupation** that breaks "unoccupied since the date of death", beyond the one 6-week disregard. **[verified: primary]**
- **Sale or assent.** Limb (a)(ii) requires that "no person is a qualifying person in any other capacity", and a buyer or beneficiary owns in their own right. **[verified: primary, by construction]** MHCLG guidance: exemption continues "so long as the dwelling … has not been transferred by the executors or administrators to the beneficiaries or sold to anyone else". **[verified: primary]**
- **Deceased tenant:** the exemption ends when the PR is no longer "liable for rent or … a licence fee, for the day". **[verified: primary]**
- **Surviving joint owner:** no Class F. The survivor is a qualifying person "whether or not jointly" in their own capacity. **[verified: primary, by construction]**
  - Malvern Hills gives the same example: "jointly owned properties where the surviving owner remains liable". **[secondary]**
  - Revision 1 cited LGSCO decision 20 011 625. **It could not be found on lgo.org.uk (404)**, so treat that citation as unverified.
- **Class E to Class F.** Class E needs an owner or tenant who "has his sole or main residence elsewhere" (a hospital or care home). It cannot continue after death, and Class F picks up from the date of death if the home was already empty. **[verified: primary, by construction]**

### 1.5 After Class F ends (England)

- **Empty-home discount.** Councils' discretion comes from **LGFA s.11A(3)–(4A)**. SI 2003/3011 reg 3 prescribes the classes it applies to. **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/14/section/11A
  - "Many councils set 0%" is **[secondary; not checked]**.
- **Long-term empty premium: LGFA s.11B.**
  - LURA 2023 s.79 substituted "1 year" for "2 years", with effect "for financial years beginning on or after 1 April 2024". https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/55/section/79 **[verified: primary]**
  - Current s.11B(8): unoccupied and substantially unfurnished "for a continuous period of at least 1 year". s.11B(9) disregards gaps of 6 weeks or less. **[verified: primary]**
- **Probate exception: Class I, SI 2003/3011 reg 13.** Inserted by **SI 2024/1007**. **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/3011/regulation/13

> 13.—(1) The class of dwellings described in this regulation ("Class I") comprises every chargeable dwelling in England— (a) that fell within Exempt Dwellings Class F; (b) in relation to which a grant of probate has been made or letters of administration issued during the period in which it has fallen within that Class; and (c) where less than one year has elapsed since the making of the grant or issue of the letters.
> (2) Where a dwelling falling within Class I is subject to a relevant transaction, it no longer falls within that Class after the day on which that transaction is completed.

  - **"Relevant transaction"** means "a transfer on sale of— (a) the freehold; or (b) the leasehold for a term of seven years or more" (SI 2003/3011 reg 2, as inserted by SI 2024/1007). https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1007/made **[verified: primary]**
    - **An assent to a beneficiary is not a transfer on sale, so Class I continues until 12 months after the grant.** Exemption Class F has already ended by then. MHCLG guidance agrees: "until the dwelling has changed owner by being sold". **[verified: primary]**
  - **Dates:**
    - SI 2024/1007 came into force on **1 November 2024** (reg 1(1)).
    - Classes G, H and I are prescribed "for each financial year beginning on or after 1st April 2025" (reg 3(4)). **[verified: primary]**
    - CTIL 5/2024: "The exceptions to the council tax premiums will have effect from 1 April 2025". https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/council-tax-information-letter-52024-exceptions-to-council-tax-premiums/council-tax-information-letter-52024-exceptions-to-council-tax-premiums **[verified: primary]**
  - Class I also applies to the **second homes premium** (s.11D). **[verified: primary]**
  - **Name clash:** premium exception "Class F" (SI 2003/3011 reg 10) is for **annexes**. **[verified: primary]**
- **How the periods fit together** **[verified: primary — MHCLG guidance]**:
  - Class I "runs concurrently with the 6-month exemption", starting at the grant.
  - The premium clock "begins when the dwelling first becomes empty rather than when an exemption ends". So once Class I ends, the premium usually applies at once unless Class G or H (actively marketed for sale or let, for up to one year) applies.
  - Councils may use discretion to extend Class I.

### 1.6 Wales — separate regime since 1 April 2026

- **Instrument.** WSI 2026/8, the Council Tax (Discounts, Disregards and Exemptions) (Wales) Regulations 2026, made 22 January 2026. **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/8/regulation/1/made
  - Reg 1(2): "These Regulations come into force on 1 April 2026", apart from some CTR-scheme provisions from 26 January 2026.
- **Welsh Class F (reg 29)** **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/8/part/5/made applies where:
  - (i) the grant "ha[s] not been obtained and the second anniversary of the date of death has not passed", **or**
  - (ii) the grant "is less than 12 months ago".
  - The same freehold, lease of 6 months or more, tenant and contract-holder limbs apply.
  - Unlike England, the time conditions apply to **all** limbs.
- **Transitional rule, Class F(3):** "Where the date of death … occurs before the coming into force of these Regulations, the date of death is taken as the date of the coming into force". So the 2-year pre-grant cap cannot end an exemption before **1 April 2028**. **[verified: primary]**
- **Drafting defect and fix.** As made, para (1)(a) omitted the "unoccupied since death" condition.
  - **WSI 2026/118** (made 24 September, laid 25 September 2026) restores "(a) has been unoccupied since the date of death of a person ('the deceased')". It also adds a 6-week disregard, both from **30 October 2026**.
  - Its explanatory note says the words were "incorrectly omitted when the 2026 Regulations were formatted for making".
  - Its Class H (ministers of religion) change applies from 1 April 2027.
  - Sources: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/118/made ; https://www.gov.wales/written-statement-council-tax-discounts-disregards-and-exemptions-wales-amendment-regulations-2026 **[verified: primary]**
- **Welsh premium exceptions.** WSI 2015/2068 sets out Classes 1–7: sale, let, annexes, armed forces, pitches and moorings, planning condition, and job-related. **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2015/2068/regulation/3
  - WSI 2026/89 adds **Class 8** (dwellings previously non-domestic) from **1 April 2027**. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/89/made **[verified: primary]**
  - **There is no probate exception in Wales, and no "Class 11/12".**
  - The Welsh long-term empty premium is under s.12A.

## 2. Refunds where the estate paid during a Class F period

### 2.1 Legal basis

- **No tax is due while the exemption applies.** s.4(1) makes tax payable only on non-exempt dwellings, and Class F has no claim requirement. **[verified: primary]**
  - Whether tax paid is "not legally due" in the restitution sense is **[inferred]**.
- **The council's duties.**
  - **Reg 8** (SI 1992/613): the authority "shall … take reasonable steps to ascertain whether any dwellings in its area will be or were exempt dwellings for any period during the year". **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/613/regulation/8
  - **Reg 9(2):** where it "has reason to believe that a particular dwelling will be or was an exempt dwelling … it shall assume … that the dwelling will be or was an exempt dwelling for that period". **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/613/regulation/9
  - Regs 14–15 are the equivalent provisions for discounts and **premiums**. **[verified: primary]**
- **Repayment: reg 31(4).** "If there has been an overpayment … (a) shall be repaid if the person so requires, or (b) in any other case shall (as the billing authority determines) either be repaid or be credited against any subsequent liability". **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/613/regulation/31
  - Reg 31 is the year-end final adjustment and contains **no time limit**.

### 2.2 Routes

1. **Ask the council** to apply Class F and repay under reg 31. **[verified: primary — legal basis above]**
   - (Revision 1 cited BCP for "no time limit and backdated". **The BCP page does not say that**, so the citation has been removed.)
2. **Appeal under LGFA 1992 s.16.** **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/14/section/16
   - s.16(4): no appeal unless "the aggrieved person serves a written notice" and a s.16(7) condition is met.
   - s.16(7)(c): the right to appeal arises when "the period of two months, beginning with the date of service of the aggrieved person's notice, has ended" without a reply. **This is not a duty on the council to reply within 2 months.**
   - **Neither s.16(4) nor s.16(6)–(8) sets any time limit on serving the notice.** (Revision 1 attributed this to Shelter, whose current page does not say so.)
   - **VTE time limits, SI 2009/2269 reg 21** (as made; later amendments not confirmed). **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/2269/regulation/21
     - Appeal within **2 months** of the council's notice (reg 21(2)), or **4 months** from the s.16(4) notice if there is no reply (reg 21(3)).
     - Late appeals only where the failure "has arisen by reason of circumstances beyond that person's control" (reg 21(6)).
   - **Representation: reg 13(1).** "A party may appoint a representative (whether a legal representative or not)". **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/2269/regulation/13
3. **Discretionary reduction, s.13A(1)(c).** Liability "may be reduced to such extent … as the billing authority … thinks fit", including to nil (s.13A(6)). **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/14/section/13A
4. **LGSCO** for maladministration. Its approach where a VTE appeal exists was not checked. **[secondary]**
5. **Restitution claim.** A *Woolwich* claim is "a claim for the recovery of taxes exacted ultra vires", as described in *AXA Insurance UK plc v HMRC* [2026] UKSC 24. https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/uksc/2026/24 **[verified: primary]**
   - Whether it lies against a council for council tax is **[inferred]**.

### 2.3 How far back? — partly resolved

- **Nothing in the regulations sets a time limit.** s.4, s.16, art 3 and regs 8, 9, 31 and 58 contain no time bar on applying an exemption to past periods or on repaying. **[verified: primary]** (The whole of SI 1992/613 was not searched.)
- **Court claims** are subject to the Limitation Act 1980 **[verified: primary]**:
  - **s.9:** 6 years for "any sum recoverable by virtue of any enactment". https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/58/section/9
  - **s.5:** 6 years for simple contract. Restitution follows s.5: *AXA v HMRC* [2026] UKSC 24, applying *FII* [2020] UKSC 47.
  - **s.32(1)(c):** for mistake, time runs from when the mistake "could with reasonable diligence have [been] discovered". https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/58/section/32
  - *AXA* (2026) revisits the *FII* test. Read it before advising.
- **Councils recovering arrears:** limited to 6 years by reg 34(3): "no application may be instituted in respect of a sum after the period of six years beginning with the day on which it became due". **[verified: primary]**
- **Still open:** whether a council can apply a 6-year cut-off to a reg 31 repayment or to a recalculation directed by the VTE.
- **HMRC's view of practice.** IHTM28192: "Personal representatives are unlikely, in practice, to get refunds of Council Tax or Rates paid for the whole of the period from the date of death". https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm28192 **[verified: primary]**
  - This is evidence that refunds are hard to obtain in practice. The same manual also misstates the exemption as "up to six months from the date of the grant".

## 3. Band challenges connected to a death

### 3.1 Legislation

- **England:** SI 2009/2270, made under "sections 24 and 113 of the Local Government Finance Act 1992". **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/2270/introduction/made
- **Wales:** SI 1993/290 as amended by WSI 2005/181 and WSI 2010/77. The Welsh list was compiled on **1 April 2005** (valuation date 1 April 2003). **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2005/181/made

### 3.2 Can a PR make a proposal?

**The PR is an interested person.** Reg 2 defines "interested person" as "(a) the owner; … (c) in relation to an exempt dwelling …, any person (other than the owner) who would be liable to pay council tax if the dwelling were not an exempt dwelling …; and (d) any other person who is a taxpayer". **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/2270/regulation/2
- Once the estate vests, the PR is the owner under s.6(5)–(6). So the PR is an interested person even while the home is exempt. **[verified: primary, by construction]**

**But the PR's proposal routes are limited** **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/2270/regulation/4:
- **Reg 4(3):** a proposal that the compiled-list band was wrong "must be made not later than the end of the period of six months beginning on the day on which the list is compiled". In England that was 1993.
- **Reg 4(4):** a person who "becomes the taxpayer" may propose within 6 months (reg 4(5)(a)), provided "he has not during that period previously been the taxpayer".
  - During Class F nobody is liable, so the PR first becomes the taxpayer only when the exemption ends. **[inferred from text]**
  - A PR who later takes as beneficiary is the same person, so gets no fresh window. **[inferred from text]**
- **Reg 4(1)(d):** a material reduction in value has no time limit (VOA: "There's no time limit on this").
- **Reg 4(1)(e) and 4(2):** a relevant VTE or High Court decision on another dwelling, within 6 months of that decision.
- **Outside these routes**, a wrong band can be fixed only by an informal review, with the listing officer altering the band under reg 3(1)(b).

**What reg 3 does.** Reg 3 ("Restrictions on alteration of valuation bands") lists the only grounds for *any* alteration **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/2270/regulation/3:
- a material increase plus a relevant transaction;
- a material reduction;
- the listing officer (LO) being "satisfied that … a different valuation band should have been determined";
- a VTE or High Court order.

**"Material reduction"** is defined in **LGFA s.24(10)**: demolition of part of the dwelling, a change in the physical state of the locality, or adaptation for a physically disabled person. **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/14/section/24

### 3.3 New owner after a probate sale

A buyer becomes the taxpayer, so **can propose within 6 months** under reg 4(4)–(5). **[verified: primary]**
- Reg 4(5)(b)–(f) exclude cases already determined by the VTE or High Court on the same facts, among others.

### 3.4 Effective date of a band reduction (reg 11)

**[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/2270/regulation/11

- **Material reduction, reg 11(3):** "shall have effect from the day on which the circumstances which caused that reduction arose". If that day cannot reasonably be ascertained, reg 11(5) applies instead: the day the proposal was served, or the day the alteration is entered in the list.
- **Compiled-list error, reg 11(7):** "an alteration made to correct an inaccuracy in a list on the day it was compiled shall have effect— (a) [where the band shown was too low] from the day on which the alteration is entered in the list; and (b) in any other case, from the day on which the list was compiled."
  - So a reduction is backdated to **1 April 1993** in England, or 1 April 2005 for the Welsh list.
- **Dwellings added to the list later** fall under reg 11(9): from the day the earlier alteration took effect, or "the day on which the list became inaccurate".
- **Increases are not backdated** (reg 11(2), (7)(a), (8)).
- **Estate angle** **[verified: primary text; practice open]**:
  - A reg 11(7)(b) correction means the deceased's own liability for earlier years was overstated.
  - Reg 58(4) entitles the PR to any excess "whether the excess arises because of his death or otherwise".
  - **How councils recalculate earlier taxpayers' years, and whether they apply any cut-off, is not settled.**
- **HMRC IHTM10161** **[verified: primary]** https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm10161
  - "The right to apply for rebanding is an asset of the estate at the date of death … the value of the right will be the amount of Council Tax that is to be refunded with an allowance to reflect uncertainty and delay. You may accept a claim for a discount made by the taxpayer or their agents against the value of the refund if it is 50% or less of the value of the Council Tax repaid."
  - **The 50% is a valuation discount, not an agent's fee.**
  - The manual also says the council "will repay overpaid Council Tax to the previous owner(s) … which may include the personal representatives of someone who has died".

### 3.5 Current VOA (now HMRC) process

**Two routes.** **[verified: primary]** https://www.gov.uk/challenge-council-tax-band (updated 14 November 2024)
- **Formal proposal** ("You have a legal right to challenge … ('make a proposal')"). Available where you have paid "for less than 6 months" or the band changed in the last 6 months. Takes "up to 4 months". Carries a right of appeal.
- **Band review.** Takes "up to 12 months". There is no appeal: "You can only appeal the decision if you had a legal right to challenge".

**Refunds.** On success the VOA will "let your local council know – your council will revise your bill and pay any refund that's due". **[verified: primary]**
- A successful review is still an "alteration", so reg 11 governs the effective date. **[verified: primary, by construction]**

**Merger into HMRC.** "On 1 April 2026, the functions of the Valuation Office Agency (VOA) will be integrated into HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC), and the Valuation Office Agency will no longer exist as an executive agency. **Case law** **[verified: primary]**
- ***R (Nicolson) v Tottenham Magistrates* [2015] EWHC 1252 (Admin)**, para 34. Magistrates must be satisfied "i) that the local authority has actually incurred those costs; ii) that the costs in question were incurred in obtaining the liability order; and iii) that it was reasonable for the local authority to incur them".
  - Costs of enforcement or "the overall administration of council tax" cannot be included (para 35).
  - https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/admin/2015/1252
  - (Revision 1's "not a penalty or source of revenue" was not the court's wording.)
- ***R (Newham LBC) v Stratford MC* [2008] EWHC 125 (Admin).** A genuine and arguable dispute about liability is "a necessary condition … but it is not a sufficient condition".
  - The order must also have been "made as a result of a substantial procedural error, defect or mishap", and the application to set it aside made "promptly".
  - The council's judicial review succeeded.
  - https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/admin/2008/125

## 9. Regulation: band and discount checking vs FCA-regulated debt advice

**The regulated activities.** In the Regulated Activities Order (SI 2001/544, inserted by SI 2013/1881), **art 39D** is debt adjusting, **art 39E** debt-counselling, **art 39F** debt-collecting and **art 39G** debt administration. **[verified: primary]**
- **Art 39E(1):** "Giving advice to a borrower about the liquidation of a debt due under a credit agreement is a specified kind of activity." https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/39E **[verified: primary]**
- **Art 39D(1):** negotiating "with the lender, on behalf of the borrower, terms for the discharge of a debt", and similar activities, "when carried on in relation to debts due under a credit agreement". **[verified: primary]**

**Council tax on its own.** Council tax is a statutory liability, not a debt under a credit agreement. So checking bands, discounts and exemptions, helping claim CTR, making proposals and s.16 appeals, and negotiating council tax arrears with the council all appear to be **outside arts 39D–39E**. **[inferred — consistent with the text; get regulatory-lawyer sign-off]**

**Where the line is crossed: PERG 17.3, Q3.3.** **[verified: primary]** https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg17/perg17s3
- "If advice is given to a debtor about his debts, some of which are not payable under a credit agreement …, that advice is regulated as long as some of the debts are due under a credit agreement…"
- "Where advice covers both the consumer credit debt and the other debt, the advice on both types of debt is likely to be debt counselling as what is done about non-consumer credit debt is likely to affect consumer credit debt, particularly if the advice does not distinguish between the two types of debt."
- **Design rule:** stop at the council tax facts, and refer on before discussing prioritisation where any credit debt exists. **[inferred]**

**Breathing Space.** Only a "debt advice provider" can start one: "(a) an authorised person who has Part 4A permission to carry on … article 39E (debt-counselling) …, or (b) a local authority" (SI 2020/1311 reg 3(1)). **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/1311/regulation/3

**Claims management.** RAO art 89G(2) lists the regulated kinds of claim: "(a) a personal injury claim; (b) a financial services or financial product claim; (c) a housing disrepair claim; (d) a claim for a specified benefit; (e) a criminal injury claim; and (f) an employment related claim". **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/89G
- "Specified benefit" (art 89F) covers industrial injuries-type benefits only.
- PERG 2.7.20M: the specified activities "are those set out in articles 89G to 89M". https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg2/perg2s7
- Council tax refunds, appeals and band challenges fall into none of these. Ingram Toft's FCA authorisation is for its other business, not a requirement for council tax work. **[inferred]**

**Legal Services Act 2007, Sch 2 para 6.** "'Probate activities' means preparing any probate papers …", where probate papers are "papers on which to found or oppose— (a) a grant of probate, or (b) a grant of letters of administration". **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/29/schedule/2
- Council tax work for an estate is not a reserved probate activity. **[inferred from the text]**

**VTE representation.** "A party may appoint a representative (whether a legal representative or not)" (SI 2009/2269 **reg 13(1)**). **[verified: primary]**

**Referral fees: CONC 8.3.11R (PS23/5).** A firm must not accept "any commission, fee or any other financial consideration, directly or indirectly, from a debt solution provider in connection with the firm referring customers". **[verified: primary]** https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/conc8/conc8s3 ; https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps23-5.pdf
- Announced **2 June 2023**. It applied immediately to new entrants, and to existing firms from **2 October 2023**.
- It applies to firms doing debt counselling that do not provide debt solutions themselves (CONC 8.3.9R), and excludes not-for-profit bodies.

# Summaries

**Part A — Deceased estates.**
- **The England rule.** Class F (SI 1992/558 art 3) exempts a home that has been **unoccupied since the death**. It lasts from death to the grant with **no time limit**, and then for up to 6 months after the grant, while the PR is the only person liable. There is a 6-week occupation disregard.
- **Premium protection.** Premium exception Class I (SI 2003/3011 reg 13, from 1 April 2025) protects the home from the empty-homes premium until 12 months after the grant. Only a **sale** ends it early; an assent does not.
- **Wales.** Since 1 April 2026 Wales caps the exemption at 2 years before the grant and 12 months after it (WSI 2026/8), with a fix taking effect 30 October 2026.
- **Refunds.**
  - Exemption needs no claim.
  - Councils must take reasonable steps to find exempt homes (regs 8–9) and repay overpayments on request (reg 31).
  - The s.16 complaint has no time limit, but court claims face a 6-year limitation period.
  - HMRC itself says PRs are "unlikely, in practice" to recover the whole period.
- **Band challenges** are narrower than they first look:
  - A PR is an interested person but gets a 6-month proposal window only once liable.
  - A buyer after a probate sale gets 6 months.
  - A compiled-list correction backdates to 1993, so the deceased's own years may be refundable to the estate (reg 58(4)).
- **Market size:**
  - 570,988 deaths (2025);
  - an estimated ~190–210k homes a year left empty by a death (inferred);
  - 295,418 grants (2025);
  - about 44% of probate grants go to personal applicants;
  - about 70% of applications are made within 6 months of death.
- **Competition.** No estate-specific recovery service exists. The nearest is a general claims firm charging 25% + VAT.

**Part B — Enforcement.**
- **Scale.** England's arrears reached **£7.4bn** (+£782m, +11.8%) at 31 March 2026, and in-year collection fell to 95.6%. About **1.72m** council tax cases were passed to bailiffs in 2025-26 (MAT FOI).
- **No official data.** There is no official count of liability orders.
- **Speed of escalation in England.** Missing one instalment can make the whole year due within about 14 days. A council can summon without a final notice. From 1 May 2026, bailiff fees start at £79 and rise to £247 at the enforcement stage.
- **Band and discount disputes cannot be argued before magistrates** (reg 57(1)), so catching them early is what matters.
- **Reforms.** England will move to 63 days, a £100 costs cap and 12 instalments by default through secondary legislation from April 2027. Wales has had a 63-day process since April 2026.
- **Unclaimed help.** About **£3.3bn** of Council Tax Support goes unclaimed across Great Britain, and **£1.7bn** of pensioner CTR in England.
- **Regulation.** Council-tax-only checking appears to sit outside FCA debt counselling, because art 39E is tied to credit agreements. Advice on mixed debts crosses the line (PERG 17.3 Q3.3).
- **Referrals.** StepChange and MAT accept organisational referrals.

# Five facts most useful to a grant application (social impact)

1. **Record arrears.** England's council tax arrears reached **£7.4bn at 31 March 2026, up £782m (11.8%) in a year**, and in-year collection fell to **95.6%** (MHCLG, revised 31 July 2026). **[verified: primary]**
2. **Bailiff exposure.** **1.72 million** council tax arrears cases were passed to bailiffs in 2025/26 across England and Wales. Only **26** councils exempt Council Tax Support recipients (Money Advice Trust FOI). **[verified: primary — MAT]**
3. **Unclaimed help.** **£3.3bn** of Council Tax Support goes unclaimed each year in Great Britain (Policy in Practice, *Missing Out 2025*). Pensioners in England miss **£1.7bn** of CTR, with only **50%** take-up (Independent Age, June 2026). **[verified: primary]**
4. **Errors are locked out of court.** A wrong band or a missed discount or exemption "may not be raised" at the liability order hearing (SI 1992/613 reg 57(1)). Meanwhile bailiff fees rose on 1 May 2026 to **£79** at compliance and **£247** at enforcement (SI 2026/366). **[verified: primary]**
5. **Bereaved households.** There were **570,988** deaths in England and Wales in 2025. **44%** of probate grants in Apr–Jun 2026 went to personal applicants rather than legal professionals. The exemption for a dead person's empty home is not applied through Tell Us Once. HMRC's own manual says executors are "unlikely, in practice" to get refunds for the whole period since death. **[verified: primary]** (The ~190–210k homes a year estimate is **[inferred]**.)

# Open legal questions for a solicitor

Questions that revision 2 answered from primary text have been removed. These remain:

1. **Refund limitation.** Can a council cap a reg 31 repayment, or a recalculation directed by the VTE, at 6 years? How do Limitation Act ss.5, 9 and 32(1)(c) apply in light of *AXA v HMRC* [2026] UKSC 24?
2. **Council's duty.** Does a Tell Us Once notification, plus the council knowing the home is empty, amount to "reason to believe" under reg 9(2), obliging it to apply Class F without a claim? What is the remedy if it keeps billing?
3. **Deceased's years after a band correction.** After a reg 11(7)(b) correction backdated to 1993, how far back can the PR recover the deceased's overpayments under regs 58(4) and 31? Do councils apply a cut-off?
4. **PR proposal window.** Is the reading of reg 4(4) correct: the PR "becomes the taxpayer" only when Class F ends, and gets no fresh window as beneficiary? Is any other route available during the exemption?
5. **FCA perimeter.** Confirm that council-tax-only checking and negotiating arrears with a council are outside RAO arts 39D–39G. Where exactly must a script stop under PERG 17.3 Q3.3?
6. **Contingency fees.** Is a percentage fee on estate refunds fair and enforceable under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 Part 2 and the DMCC Act 2024? What level is defensible against MSE's "not much more than 25%"?
7. **Authority and capacity.** What written authority do councils and HMRC (for banding) accept from an agent acting for a PR? When are an LPA, deputyship or appointeeship needed for SMI claims (Mental Capacity Act 2005)? What UK GDPR Art 9 condition applies?
8. **Liability orders.** Where an exemption is established after a liability order, reg 57(2) treats the sum as paid. Are summons costs recoverable? Is *Newham*'s "substantial procedural error" test ever met in such cases?
9. **Welsh transition.** For deaths before 1 April 2026, does the deemed date of death in Class F(3) interact with WSI 2026/118's restored "unoccupied since the date of death" condition? Is that date the real death or the deemed one?
