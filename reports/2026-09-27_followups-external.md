## F1 (legal half) — Can a billing authority cap a council tax refund at 6 years?

**Answer: a flat "6 years only" refund rule is not clearly lawful, and how far back an estate can reclaim depends on how the claim is framed.** [verified: primary on statute/cases; application inferred]

- The Limitation Act 1980 bars an **"action"** (court proceedings), not the underlying duty. SI 1992/613 reg 31 imposes a repayment duty with **no statutory time limit**, so a 6-year cap is the council asserting the limitation defence it *would* have to a court claim — not a limit in reg 31 itself. Whether limitation even bites a claim to enforce the reg 31 duty (versus a common-law restitution claim) is **unsettled**.
- **Limitation Act 1980** (read on legislation.gov.uk): s.9(1) — 6 years for "any sum recoverable by virtue of any enactment"; s.5 — 6 years for simple contract (restitution treated analogously); s.32(1)(c) — for "relief from the consequences of a mistake", time does not run until the claimant "could with reasonable diligence" have discovered it. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/58/section/9 ; /section/5 ; /section/32 [verified: primary]
- **Two framings, two very different look-backs:**
  - **Mistake-based restitution** (the natural fit for an unclaimed Class F exemption): s.32(1)(c) postpones the clock. Per *Test Claimants in the FII Group Litigation v HMRC* [2020] UKSC 47 and *AXA Insurance UK plc v HMRC* [2026] UKSC 24 (both read), time runs from when the claimant "could have realised that [they] had a worthwhile claim" — potentially **well beyond 6 years from payment**. https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/uksc/2026/24 [verified: primary]
  - ***Woolwich* unlawful-demand restitution** ([1993] AC 70): hard 6 years from payment, **no** s.32(1)(c) extension. [secondary]
- **The genuine gap:** no council-tax-specific limitation authority, and no VTE or LGSCO decision on the maximum look-back for a reg 31 repayment, was located. A solicitor must confirm (i) whether limitation applies to the reg 31 duty at all, (ii) the accrual date, (iii) whether s.32(1)(c) mistake applies to a Class F backdating. This is exactly what the F4 brief puts to counsel.
- **Practice [secondary]:** advice sources say some councils cap refunds at 6 years, others go back to 1993; the cap is driven by ~6-year record-retention, not a clear statutory rule. Note F2 below found **no council Class F page states any time limit**.

## F2 — How councils actually handle Class F and refunds (≈22-authority sample)

Full table (council-by-council) in the working notes. Headlines [verified: primary for the 17 councils read directly; secondary for 5 bot-blocked]:

- **Duration wording is near-uniform and legally correct for England.** Every page states the two-phase rule: exempt from the date of death (unlimited) until the grant, then up to 6 months more, ending earlier on sale/transfer/re-let/occupation. **No English council repeats the out-of-date "6 months after grant" error** (that was a Welsh-rule problem; "6 months after grant" is correct for England). Several correctly add the "≤6-week occupation ignored" nuance.
- **The big divergence is process, not rule.** Only **Newcastle** clearly treats a **Tell Us Once** notification as enough (auto-applies, auto-refunds). Greenwich and North Northants act on a single death notification. The **majority require a separate claim/application** (Leeds, Durham, Portsmouth, Bath & NES, South Ribble, Westminster, Liverpool, Bristol, North Norfolk). So **Tell Us Once alone is generally not sufficient** — a point of real friction for bereaved families.
- **Backdating** happens by operation of law (exempt "since the death") but is rarely spelled out; explicit "backdated / tax refunded" wording is rare (Newcastle clearest).
- **Evidence** asked is light and inconsistent: most ask nothing up front; Barnet wants a death certificate; Bradford lists grant/will/POA but only to authorise a **refund**.
- **No sampled council page states any refund time limit or 6-year cap.** The 6-year idea appears only in general debt commentary [secondary], consistent with F1's "not a clear rule".
- **Blocked (host 403/Cloudflare):** Birmingham, Manchester, Camden, Barnet, East Lindsey own pages; Commons Library CBP-10651. Captured via search where possible.

## F9 — Is there an official national count of council tax liability orders (England)?

**Answer: no. There is no official national count.** [verified: primary]

- **MHCLG's collection-rates release contains no "liability order" figure** — it measures only in-year collection rate and arrears (confirmed by reading the 2024-25 release). https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/collection-rates-for-council-tax-and-non-domestic-rates-in-england-2024-to-2025/collection-rates-for-council-tax-and-non-domestic-rates-in-england-2024-to-2025 [verified: primary]
- **The "2.4 million (2024/25)" figure is unsourced/circular.** It traces to an SEO page (counciltaxadvisors.co.uk) that attributes it to "GOV.UK council tax collection data" which does not contain it. The Money Advice Trust's *Lost in the system* (Feb 2026) does not state it either. **Drop it.** [verified: primary]
- No MoJ/HMCTS published series counts council tax liability orders. [inferred + secondary]
- **Best-provenanced estimate: ~2.3 million liability orders, England, 2018/19 — Citizens Advice, from FOI requests to councils.** An estimate, not an official statistic. [secondary]
- **Blocked:** questions-statements.parliament.uk (Cloudflare) for the confirming PQ; committees/publications.parliament.uk (403) for the Citizens Advice written evidence.

- **Grant-to-sale:** no official dataset (HMLR Price Paid has no probate flag). Best-sourced estimates [verified: primary — company pages]:
  - Kings Court Trust: after the grant, selling "can take **three to six months** on average". https://www.kctrust.co.uk/help/how-long-after-a-house-is-sold-before-your-receive-an-inheritance

## F11 — Rebuilt "owner-occupied homes emptied by a death per year"

**Rebuilt estimate: ~171,000–261,000 a year, central ~200,000.** Now grounded in sourced inputs rather than bare assumptions. [verified: primary inputs; arithmetic inferred]

Model: `Deaths × [care-share × (care owners) + (1−care-share) × (last-adult) × (owner-occupation)]`, with:
- Deaths 570,988 (2025); care-home death share 23.1% (2022).
- **Marital status of deaths** (ONS *Mortality by marital status*, parsed): **62% of 65+ deaths are non-married** (widowed 44.9%, divorced 10.3%, single 6.9%). https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/birthsdeathsandmarriages/deaths/datasets/mortalitybymaritalstatusinenglandandwales [verified: primary]
- **Living alone** (Census 2021): women 65+ 40.9%, all 65+ ~30.1%, centenarians 41.6%. [verified: primary]
- Owner-occupation 65+ 74–79% (EHS) [secondary]. "Last adult in home" set at central 0.50 (range 0.45–0.58); care-home owners 0.25/0.35/0.45.

Results: **low ~171k, central ~204k, high ~261k.**

**Cross-checks (both hold):**
1. Central 204k = **69% of the 295,418 grants of representation** in 2025 — plausible (many estates have no property, or a surviving co-owner). [verified anchor; ratio inferred]
2. Class F stock 124,064 ÷ 204k ≈ **7.3 months** implied average Class F duration — matches the death→grant timeline (~70% apply within 6 months of death, plus the pre-grant exempt period). Strong independent corroboration that the flow is ~200k. [inferred]

**Weakest input:** the share of care-home decedents who still own an empty home (no direct data; ~37% self-funder share is only a proxy).

## F4 — FCA perimeter / regulatory brief for the checking-and-refund service

Delivered as a standalone solicitor brief: **`reports/2026-09-27_fca-perimeter-solicitor-brief.md`**. It contains 8 confirm-or-correct questions with a "current understanding" (tagged) under each, and a red-flag list. Headlines [verified: primary on the statute/Handbook; load-bearing conclusions flagged inferred for counsel]:

- **Core council tax work appears outside the FCA perimeter** — RAO arts 39D–39G are all tied to a "credit agreement"; council tax is a statutory liability. (Confirm.)
- **The line is crossed on mixed debts** (PERG 17.3 Q3.3): advising a customer who also has credit debts, especially on which to prioritise, is likely regulated debt-counselling. Design rule: stop at the council tax facts and refer on; don't open Breathing Space.
- **A contingency fee on a refund is not FCA-regulated claims management** (RAO art 89G(2) list doesn't include it) — **but it is squarely within consumer law**: CRA 2015 Part 2 (unfair terms), DMCC Act 2024 (misleading **omission** if "you can do this free" is buried), and distance-selling cancellation rules. Defensible package: fee at/near the ~25% MSE benchmark, prominent "free alternative" disclosure, a cap, compliant cooling-off terms.
- **Estates:** council tax work is not a reserved "probate activity" (LSA 2007 Sch 2 para 6); a PR's letter of authority is needed.
- **Capacity (SMI clients):** capacity is presumed and decision-specific; where absent, an LPA/deputy is needed — a DWP appointee likely can't sign a private contingency retainer.
- **Three biggest risks:** mixed-debt drift into debt-counselling; contingency-fee consumer-law exposure; capacity/authority for impaired clients.
