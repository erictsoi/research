# Instruction to counsel — UK regulatory perimeter for a council tax band / discount / exemption checking and refund-recovery service

**Instructing party:** [client / promoter of the proposed service]
**Prepared:** 27 September 2026
**Jurisdiction:** England and Wales (England unless Wales noted)
**Purpose:** To obtain a regulatory-perimeter opinion before launch. The service will (a) check council tax bands, discounts and exemptions, (b) lodge band challenges (VOA/HMRC), discount/exemption claims and s.16 appeals, (c) negotiate arrears/refunds with billing authorities, and (d) recover refunds — for **living taxpayers** and for **deceased estates** — on a contingency ("no win, no fee") basis.

## How to read this brief

This is a **drafting + light-verification** instruction. Most of the underlying legislative text has already been read against primary sources (legislation.gov.uk and the FCA Handbook) in a prior research exercise and re-confirmed in this session. Each legal reference is tagged:

- **[verified: primary]** — the primary text (legislation.gov.uk revised text, or FCA Handbook) was read this session or in the cited prior report; key wording quoted.
- **[secondary]** — a secondary source (guidance, trade body, charity, company page).
- **[inferred]** — instructing party's or researcher's reasoning applied to verified text; **this is precisely what counsel is asked to confirm.**

Counsel is asked to **confirm or correct** the "current understanding" under each question, not to start cold. Where the understanding is tagged **[inferred]**, that is the live risk.

Cross-reference: the verified FCA/RAO/LSA findings sit in the instructing party's research file `reports/2026-09-26_deceased-estates-and-enforcement.md` §9 (and §§1–3, 8 for the substantive council tax law). Blocked sources noted at the end.

# PART 1 — THE QUESTION SET FOR COUNSEL

## Q1 — FCA perimeter: is core council tax work outside regulated debt activity?

**Yes/no:** Is checking a band, lodging a band challenge/proposal, making a discount or exemption claim (including SMI/disabled), or bringing a s.16 LGFA 1992 appeal, **outside** the FCA regulatory perimeter because council tax is a statutory liability and not a "debt due under a credit agreement"?

Sub-issues:
1. Do RAO (SI 2001/544) arts **39D** (debt adjusting), **39E** (debt-counselling), **39F** (debt-collecting) and **39G** (debt administration) all require the debt to be "due under a credit agreement", such that council tax falls outside all four?
2. Does the analysis differ for **debt-collecting/adjusting on behalf of the taxpayer** (recovering a refund from a council) versus advising the taxpayer?
3. Is any FCA authorisation nonetheless prudent (e.g. if the service ever touches consumer-credit debt)?

## Q2 — FCA perimeter: the mixed-debt line (PERG 17.3)

**Yes/no:** Where the **same customer also has consumer-credit debts**, does giving advice that touches both the council tax liability and the credit debt convert the whole engagement into **regulated debt-counselling**? Exactly where must the customer-facing script stop?

Sub-issues:
1. Confirm the PERG 17.3 Q3.3 rule: advice on a mix of debts is regulated "as long as some of the debts are due under a credit agreement", and advice on both types "is likely to be debt counselling" because what is done about the non-credit debt affects the credit debt.
2. Is a hard script boundary — "we advise only on the council tax facts (band/discount/exemption/CTR/arrears with the council); we do not advise on prioritisation across your other debts and will refer you to free FCA-authorised or exempt debt advice" — sufficient to stay outside art 39E?
3. Does merely **identifying** that a customer has credit debts, or **referring** them on, cross the line?
4. Does helping a customer access a **Breathing Space** moratorium require authorisation (only a "debt advice provider" with art 39E permission, or a local authority, can start one — SI 2020/1311 reg 3(1))?

## Q3 — Is negotiating a council tax arrears arrangement with a billing authority a regulated activity?

**Yes/no:** Is negotiating an instalment/arrears arrangement, or a refund set-off, with a **billing authority** (not a lender) a regulated activity (debt adjusting art 39D / debt-collecting art 39F)?

Sub-issues:
1. Confirm arts 39D/39F are tied to "a credit agreement" and council tax arrears owed to a council are outside.
2. Does it matter that the counterparty is a public authority rather than a commercial lender?
3. Does acting after a **liability order** (enforcement stage) change anything?

## Q4 — Is a contingency fee on a council tax refund a regulated claims-management activity, or otherwise regulated?

**Yes/no:** Is charging a "no win, no fee" percentage on a council tax refund a **regulated claims-management activity** under RAO art 89G?

Sub-issues:
1. Confirm the RAO art **89G(2)** closed list of regulated "kinds of claim" — personal injury, financial services/product, housing disrepair, specified benefit, criminal injury, employment — and that **council tax refunds/rebanding/appeals fall into none** ("specified benefit", art 89F, being industrial-injuries-type benefits only).
2. Given that, is a council tax refund contingency fee regulated by the FCA at all? If not by the FCA, is it caught by any other regime (see Q5)?
3. Is there any read-across from an FCA-authorised claims firm also doing this work (e.g. Ingram Toft, FRN 831386) that would imply authorisation is required? (Understanding: its authorisation is for its other business, not required for council tax work — confirm.)

## Q5 — Consumer-law limits on the fee and terms

**Yes/no:** Is a percentage contingency fee (and its disclosure) on a council tax refund **fair and enforceable** under UK consumer law?

Sub-issues:
1. **Consumer Rights Act 2015 Part 2** (unfair terms): is the fee a "core"/price term excluded from the fairness assessment under s.64 **only if** transparent (plain, intelligible, legible) and prominent? If it fails that, is it assessable for fairness under s.62 (significant imbalance contrary to good faith) and, if unfair, "not binding on the consumer"?
2. **DMCC Act 2024** (unfair commercial practices, in force 6 April 2025): does the marketing/onboarding risk a **misleading action** (s.226), **misleading omission** (s.227 — failing to give material information such as that the customer can do it free themselves via the VOA/HMRC and the council), **aggressive practice** (s.228), or breach of **professional diligence** (s.229)?
3. **Cancellation rights** — Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 (SI 2013/3134): for a distance/off-premises contract, does the 14-day cancellation right (regs 29–30) apply, and does starting work in the cancellation period require the consumer's **express request** (reg 36) before any fee can be charged? What happens if the refund lands during the cooling-off period?
4. **Defensible fee level and disclosure:** benchmark against MSE guidance that a rebanding fee "shouldn't be much more than 25% of what you win" and market comparables (Challenge My Band 30%; Ingram Toft 25% + VAT capped £10,000). What level, cap, and pre-contract disclosure (including "you can do this for free") are defensible?

## Q6 — Deceased estates: reserved legal activities (LSA 2007)

**Yes/no:** Does any of the estate-facing work touch a **reserved "probate activity"** under the Legal Services Act 2007?

Sub-issues:
1. Confirm LSA 2007 **Sch 2 para 6**: "probate activities" means preparing "papers on which to found or oppose a grant of probate or letters of administration" — i.e. the grant application only.
2. Confirm that council tax checking, claims, appeals and refund recovery **for an estate** are not reserved probate activities (and are not reserved instrument preparation or litigation).
3. What **written authority** does a billing authority, and the VOA (now within HMRC from 1 April 2026), accept from an agent acting for a personal representative (PR)? Is a signed letter of authority from the PR sufficient, and does the agent need sight of the grant?
4. Does acting for the estate before a grant is obtained raise any authority issue (limb (2)(a)(i) Class F cases where no one is yet a qualifying person)?

## Q7 — Capacity: SMI / significant-cognitive-impairment clients (MCA 2005)

**Yes/no:** Can an SMI / significantly-cognitively-impaired client **validly authorise** the agent, and when is an LPA / deputyship / DWP appointeeship required?

Sub-issues:
1. Apply the MCA 2005 s.1 principles — presumption of capacity (s.1(2)), "unwise decision" not equalling incapacity (s.1(4)) — and the s.2(1)/s.3 test to the specific decision of engaging an agent on contingency terms. Capacity is **decision-specific**.
2. When the client lacks capacity to enter the retainer, who can sign: an attorney under a **property and affairs LPA** (s.9), a Court of Protection **deputy** (s.16), or a DWP **appointee** (noting an appointee's authority is limited to benefits/DWP matters and may not extend to a private contingency retainer or a council tax refund)?
3. The SMI **disregard/exemption** claim itself requires a doctor's certificate (LGFA 1992 Sch 1 para 2) — is the agent handling that materially different, for capacity purposes, from the fee retainer?
4. Safeguarding: what onboarding checks and vulnerable-consumer treatment should the service adopt so that contracts with impaired clients are not voidable and the firm is not exploiting vulnerability (also feeds DMCC s.228)?

## Q8 — Data protection (UK GDPR)

**Yes/no:** For SMI/disability claims involving **health data**, does the service have a valid Art 6 lawful basis and an Art 9 special-category condition, both when acting **direct** and when acting **as agent** for a PR or third party?

Sub-issues:
1. Art 6(1) basis — likely (b) contract or (f) legitimate interests; (c) legal obligation where a statutory disclosure applies.
2. Art 9(2) condition for health data — Art 9(2)(a) **explicit consent**, or Art 9(2)(f) **legal claims** (establishing/exercising/defending a legal claim); assess which is more robust for a claim/appeal that may not reach a court/tribunal, and whether the DPA 2018 Sch 1 substantial-public-interest routes assist.
3. **Controller/processor and agency:** is the service a controller in its own right or acting for the client; what is the position where the client lacks capacity (consent given by attorney/deputy)?
4. Impact of the **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025** "recognised legitimate interest" basis (Art 6(1)(ea), in force 5 February 2026) — does it assist here?

# PART 2 — CURRENT UNDERSTANDING (for counsel to confirm or correct)

## On Q1 (core council tax work outside FCA perimeter)
**Understanding: outside the perimeter.** The RAO debt activities are all tied to a "credit agreement":
- Art **39E(1)**: "Giving advice to a borrower about the liquidation of a debt due under **a credit agreement** is a specified kind of activity." **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/39E
- Art **39D(1)**: negotiating with the lender "terms for the discharge of a debt … when carried on in relation to debts due under **a credit agreement**." **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/39D
- Arts 39F (debt-collecting) and 39G (debt administration) are similarly tied to a credit agreement. **[verified: primary]**

Council tax is a **statutory liability**, not a debt under a credit agreement. So band checks, band challenges, discount/exemption claims, CTR help, s.16 appeals, and refund recovery from the council **appear to fall outside arts 39D–39G**. **[inferred — consistent with the text; counsel to confirm]** (Ref file §9.)

## On Q2 (mixed-debt line, PERG 17.3)
**Understanding: the line is crossed once advice touches both a credit debt and the council tax debt.** PERG 17.3 Q3.3:
> "If advice is given to a debtor about his debts, some of which are not payable under a credit agreement …, that advice is regulated as long as some of the debts are due under a credit agreement… Where advice covers both the consumer credit debt and the other debt, the advice on both types of debt is likely to be debt counselling as what is done about non-consumer credit debt is likely to affect consumer credit debt, particularly if the advice does not distinguish between the two types of debt." **[verified: primary]** https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg17/perg17s3

**Design rule (proposed):** the script stops at the council tax facts and refers on before any cross-debt prioritisation where the customer has credit debts. **[inferred]** Breathing Space can only be commenced by an art 39E-authorised "debt advice provider" or a local authority (SI 2020/1311 reg 3(1)). **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/1311/regulation/3 — so the service should **not** offer to open a moratorium itself.

## On Q3 (negotiating arrears with a billing authority)
**Understanding: outside arts 39D/39F**, because the debt is council tax owed to a public authority under statute, not a credit-agreement debt, and the counterparty is not a lender. **[inferred — consistent with the credit-agreement wording of 39D/39F; counsel to confirm]** Note this is an inference, not verified authority.

## On Q4 (claims-management)
**Understanding: not a regulated claims-management activity.** RAO art **89G(2)** lists the regulated kinds of claim:
> "(a) a personal injury claim; (b) a financial services or financial product claim; (c) a housing disrepair claim; (d) a claim for a specified benefit; (e) a criminal injury claim; and (f) an employment related claim." **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/89G

"Specified benefit" (art 89F) is industrial-injuries-type benefits only; PERG 2.7.20M confirms the specified activities are "those set out in articles 89G to 89M." **[verified: primary]** https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg2/perg2s7 A **council tax refund is not within any listed kind of claim**, so a contingency fee on it is not FCA-regulated claims management. **[inferred]** An FCA-authorised firm doing this work is authorised for its other business, not because council tax requires it. **[inferred]** (Ref file §9.)

## On Q5 (consumer law)
**Understanding: unregulated by the FCA but squarely within general consumer law; the fee is defensible only if transparent, prominent, and honestly marketed.**
- **CRA 2015 s.62:** a term is unfair if "contrary to the requirement of good faith, it causes a significant imbalance in the parties' rights and obligations … to the detriment of the consumer", and an unfair term "is not binding on the consumer". **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/part/2
- **CRA 2015 s.64:** the price/main-subject-matter exclusion from the fairness assessment applies **only** where the term is transparent ("plain and intelligible language and … legible") and prominent ("brought to the consumer's attention in such a way that an average consumer would be aware of the term"). A poorly disclosed contingency fee loses that protection and becomes assessable for fairness. **[verified: primary]**
- **DMCC Act 2024** unfair commercial practices — **in force 6 April 2025** (SI 2025/272): s.225 general prohibition; s.226 misleading actions; **s.227 misleading omissions** (omitting "material information" the average consumer needs); s.228 aggressive practices; s.229 professional diligence. **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/part/4 The **material omission risk** is real: not telling the consumer they can challenge the band free via the VOA/HMRC and claim discounts/exemptions free from the council. **[inferred]**
- **Cancellation:** SI 2013/3134 — 14-day right to cancel a distance/off-premises contract (regs 29–30); payment for services begun in the cooling-off period only on the consumer's **express request** (reg 36). **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/contents
- **Defensible level:** MSE benchmark ~25% ("shouldn't be much more than 25% of what you win"); market comparables 25%–30%. A fee at/near 25% (+ VAT), with a prominent pre-contract statement that the consumer can do it free, a cap, and compliant cancellation terms, is the defensible package. **[secondary/inferred]** (Ref file §5.)

## On Q6 (LSA 2007 reserved activities)
**Understanding: no reserved probate activity is engaged.** LSA 2007 Sch 2 para 6: "'probate activities' means preparing any probate papers …" being "papers on which to found or oppose— (a) a grant of probate, or (b) a grant of letters of administration." **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/29/schedule/2 Council tax work for an estate is **not** within that (nor reserved instrument preparation or the conduct of litigation). **[inferred]** VTE representation is expressly open to non-lawyers: "A party may appoint a representative (whether a legal representative or not)" — SI 2009/2269 reg 13(1). **[verified: primary]** Councils/VOA(HMRC) practice on accepting a PR's letter of authority is **[not verified — counsel/enquiry needed]**. (Ref file §9 and §2.2.)

## On Q7 (capacity, MCA 2005)
**Understanding: capacity is presumed and decision-specific; an impaired client may still validly authorise the agent, but robust checks and, where capacity is absent, an LPA/deputy are needed.**
- MCA 2005 **s.1(2)**: "A person must be assumed to have capacity unless it is established that he lacks capacity." **s.1(4)**: "A person is not to be treated as unable to make a decision merely because he makes an unwise decision." **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/section/1
- **s.2(1)**: "a person lacks capacity in relation to a matter if at the material time he is unable to make a decision for himself in relation to the matter because of an impairment of, or a disturbance in the functioning of, the mind or brain." **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/section/2
- Where capacity to enter the retainer is absent, authority must come from a property-and-affairs **LPA** (s.9) or a Court of Protection **deputy** (s.16); a DWP **appointee's** authority is confined to benefits and likely does not cover a private contingency retainer or a council tax refund. **[verified: primary for ss.1/2/9/16; appointee-scope point inferred]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/contents SMI status for the tax **disregard** (doctor's certificate, LGFA Sch 1 para 2) does **not** itself mean the client lacks capacity to contract. **[verified: primary — Sch 1 para 2; inference noted]**

## On Q8 (UK GDPR)
**Understanding: contract or legitimate interests for Art 6; explicit consent or legal-claims for Art 9.**
- Art **6(1)**: bases include (a) consent, (b) contract/pre-contract steps, (c) legal obligation, (f) legitimate interests. **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/6 Likely (b) and/or (f). A new (ea) "recognised legitimate interest" was added by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (in force 5 Feb 2026). **[verified: primary — noted on legislation.gov.uk]**
- Art **9(1)** prohibits processing health data unless a 9(2) condition applies; the workable conditions here are **9(2)(a) explicit consent** and **9(2)(f)** "establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims". **[verified: primary]** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/9 Explicit consent is the cleaner basis at intake; 9(2)(f) supports processing during a contested claim/appeal. Where the client lacks capacity, consent must come from the attorney/deputy. **[inferred]**

# PART 3 — RED-FLAG LIST (most likely to bite)

1. **Mixed-debt drift into regulated debt-counselling (Q2).** The single biggest authorisation risk. If front-line staff discuss the customer's credit debts alongside council tax — even informally, or by advising which to pay first — the whole conversation is likely debt-counselling (art 39E) per PERG 17.3 Q3.3, requiring FCA authorisation. **Mitigation: a hard script boundary and a documented referral-out protocol; do not open Breathing Space; train and monitor.** Note also the **CONC 8.3.11R referral-fee ban (PS23/5)** — a firm doing debt counselling must not take a fee from a debt-solution provider for referrals. **[verified: primary]** https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/conc8/conc8s3

2. **Contingency-fee consumer-law exposure (Q5).** Even outside FCA regulation, the fee is exposed under CRA 2015 Part 2 (unfair/non-binding if not transparent and prominent, s.62/s.64) and DMCC Act 2024 (misleading **omission** if the "you can do this free" fact is buried, s.227; professional diligence, s.229). Distance-selling cancellation and the reg 36 "express request" trap can leave the firm unable to charge for work done in the cooling-off period. **Mitigation: fee at/near the ~25% MSE benchmark, prominent "free alternative" disclosure, a cap, and compliant cooling-off terms.**

3. **Capacity and authority for SMI / cognitively-impaired clients (Q7).** Contracting on contingency terms with an impaired client risks a voidable retainer, a safeguarding/vulnerability failing, and DMCC aggressive-practice/undue-influence exposure. Presumption of capacity is decision-specific; a DWP appointee likely **cannot** validly sign a private contingency retainer. **Mitigation: capacity-aware onboarding, insist on LPA/deputy where capacity is absent, vulnerable-consumer policy.**

4. **Estate authority (Q6, secondary flag).** No reserved probate activity is engaged, but the service must hold a proper **letter of authority from the PR** and understand what the council/VOA(HMRC) will accept; acting before a grant (Class F limb (2)(a)(i)) needs care about who can authorise.
